Paper Review 01: AMDS (Olukola & Rahimi, 2026)

Title: AMDS: Attack-Aware Multi-Stage Defense System for Network Intrusion Detection with Two-Stage Adaptive Weight Learning
Authors: Oluseyi Olukola and Nick Rahimi
Year & Venue: 2026 | IEEE Transactions on Information Forensics and Security / arXiv:2602.xxxxx
Category: Part 1 — Similar Methodology / Direct Peer

1. Paper Overview

  • Domain: Network Intrusion Detection Systems (NIDS), Adversarial Machine Learning, Dynamic Loss Scheduling.
  • One-Paragraph Summary: Olukola and Rahimi propose AMDS, a two-stage defensive training architecture designed to resolve the catastrophic clean accuracy degradation commonly seen in adversarially trained tabular NIDS. Stage 1 trains an initial neural representation backbone on clean network traffic to establish tight, separated feature clusters. Stage 2 dynamically injects gradient-based perturbations (PGD-20) while adaptively adjusting the loss weighting between clean cross-entropy and adversarial loss based on mini-batch loss gradients. The authors claim this dynamic loss weighting preserves clean traffic detection while hardening the classifier against multi-step evasion.
  • Main Research Problem: Traditional adversarial training applies static equal weighting or extreme adversarial weighting, causing the model to forget clean traffic patterns, inflating False Positive Rates (FPR) in production networks.
  • Main Motivation: Cloud enterprise networks require high throughput and minimal false alarm rates; an IDS that drops clean accuracy from 98% to 80% to survive attacks is operationally unusable.

2. Research Gap & Novelty

  • What Existing Research Was Missing: Existing defenses either used static loss ratios ($\alpha=0.5$) throughout training or relied on static pre-generated adversarial datasets that fail to track shifting decision boundaries.
  • Claimed vs. Actual Novelty:
    • Claimed: A novel adaptive weight controller that dynamically adjusts $\alpha_t$ at each iteration based on the ratio of adversarial to clean loss gradients.
    • Actual: An empirical dynamic scheduler akin to focal loss or curriculum learning applied to adversarial training. It helps training stability, but still operates across all raw tabular features without attribution pruning.

3. Previous Work & Theoretical Foundations

  • Madry et al. (2018): Min-max formulation $\min_\theta \mathbb{E} [\max_\delta \mathcal{L}(\theta, x+\delta, y)]$. AMDS builds directly on this by splitting the objective into a two-stage curriculum.
  • Zhang et al. (2019) TRADES: Trade-off between clean and robust accuracy. AMDS attempts a heuristic tabular equivalent of TRADES.

4. Dataset and Data Preparation

  • Dataset: CSE-CIC-IDS2018 and CIC-IDS2017.
  • Size: Evaluated on ~2.5 million sampled flows across 78 numeric attributes.
  • Classes: Binary (Benign vs. Attack) and 7-class multi-class classification.
  • Preprocessing: MinMax normalization to $[0, 1]$, imputation of missing values, removal of zero-variance features. Class balancing performed via weighted loss penalties rather than SMOTE.

5. Methodology & Architecture

+-----------------------------------------------------------------------------+
|                               AMDS PIPELINE                                 |
+-----------------------------------------------------------------------------+
  [ Clean Preprocessed Flows ]
               |
               v
   +-----------------------+
   |   STAGE 1: WARMUP     | ---> Train Backbone for 15 epochs on clean data
   +-----------------------+      Establishes stable representation manifold
               |
               v
   +-----------------------+
   |  STAGE 2: ADAPTIVE    | ---> Generate 20-step PGD on-the-fly per batch
   |  DYNAMIC HARDENING    |      Compute alpha_t = f(grad_adv / grad_clean)
   +-----------------------+      Loss = (1 - alpha_t)*L_clean + alpha_t*L_adv
               |
               v
   [ Hardened AMDS Model ] ---> Restores 89.4% PGD-20 accuracy, 94.8% clean

6. Model & Algorithm Details

  • Architecture: 4-layer MLP (256-128-64-32) with ELU activations, BatchNorm, and Dropout (0.25).
  • Optimizer: AdamW ($\text{lr} = 0.001$, weight decay = $10^{-4}$).
  • Attack Parameters: PGD with 20 steps, step size $\alpha = \epsilon / 4$, $\epsilon = 0.05$ in normalized space.

7. Experiments & Results

  • Clean Accuracy: 94.8% (down 2.1% from undefended baseline 96.9%).
  • White-Box Attack (PGD-20): 89.4% accuracy (ASR: 10.6%).
  • FGSM Attack: 91.2% accuracy.
  • Training Overhead: +140% training time compared to standard training due to full-dimension 20-step backprop.

8. Strengths vs. Weaknesses

  • Strengths: Explicit recognition of the two-stage curriculum (clean warmup before attack injection); prevents early representation collapse.
  • Weaknesses:
    1. Computes PGD across all 78 raw features, introducing severe computational overhead.
    2. Did not evaluate against adaptive attacks (APGD or Carlini-Wagner margin loss).
    3. No feature attribution analysis; cannot explain why certain flow features resist perturbation.

9. Takeaways for Our Thesis (Phase2Net Comparison)

  • Our Edge: Phase2Net achieves 90.13% accuracy against 100-step PGD (vs. AMDS's 89.4% on 20-step PGD) and 96.19% clean accuracy (+1.39% higher than AMDS).
  • Why We Win: AMDS trains over all 78 noisy dimensions. Phase2Net uses SHAP stability $S(f)$ to prune the input to 27 robust features before training, eliminating gradient noise and accelerating GPU throughput.

Paper Review 02: Adversarial Threats to Cloud IDS (Holla et al., 2025)

Title: Adversarial Threats to Cloud IDS: Robust Defense With Adversarial Training and Feature Selection
Authors: Hariprasad Holla, Shashidhar Reddy Polepalli, Arun Ambika Sasikumar
Year & Venue: 2025 | IEEE Access, Vol. 13, pp. 84992–85003 (DOI: 10.1109/ACCESS.2025.3567038)
Category: Part 1 — Direct Foundational Baseline Paper

1. Paper Overview

  • Domain: Cloud Cybersecurity, Tabular NIDS, Adversarial Machine Learning, XAI (SHAP).
  • One-Paragraph Summary: Holla et al. evaluate deep learning intrusion detection on the CSE-CIC-IDS2018 benchmark against FGSM, PGD, DeepFool, and Square Attack. Observing that attacks degrade baseline accuracy from 85% to 50%–60%, they propose a dual-stage defense combining offline static adversarial training (augmenting clean data with 10k FGSM, 10k PGD, and 10k DeepFool samples generated once) and SHAP feature selection (selecting top 15 features). The defense claims to restore accuracy to 88% under gradient attacks and 84% under Square Attack with a 25% training overhead (3.75 hrs vs 3.0 hrs).
  • Main Research Problem: Deep learning models deployed in cloud IDS learn brittle traffic features that adversaries can evade using subtle perturbations without disrupting malicious payloads.
  • Main Motivation: Cloud multi-tenancy means a single compromised IDS sensor can compromise multiple tenants; defenses must balance robustness, latency, and explainability.

2. Research Gap & Novelty

  • What Existing Research Was Missing: Prior works treated adversarial training and feature selection independently.
  • Claimed vs. Actual Novelty:
    • Claimed: A novel dual-layered defense integrating adversarial training and SHAP interpretability.
    • Actual: Standard offline static data augmentation combined with a heuristic feature filter. Adversarial training is not dynamic min-max optimization.

3. Dataset and Preprocessing

  • Dataset: CSE-CIC-IDS2018 (AWS cloud network capture, 16M+ flows, 80 features).
  • Task: Binary classification (Benign vs. Malicious).
  • Preprocessing: Deduplication, null imputation, MinMax scaling to $[0, 1]$, SMOTE minority oversampling, and Mutual Information (MI) filtering.
  • Flaw: Authors retain socket metadata (Src Port, Dst Port), allowing shortcut learning.

4. Methodology & Architecture

+-----------------------------------------------------------------------------+
|                            HOLLA ET AL. PIPELINE                            |
+-----------------------------------------------------------------------------+
  [ Raw CSE-CIC-IDS2018 Flows ] ---> Preprocessing (SMOTE, MinMax, MI)
               |
               v
  [ Train Initial Baseline DNN ] ---> Generate Static Adversarial Batches
               |                      (10k FGSM, 10k PGD, 1k-10k DeepFool)
               v
  [ SHAP Stability Selection ]   ---> Compute S_f = 1 - |SHAP_D - SHAP_Dadv|
               |                      Select Top T = 15 Features
               v
  [ Static Retraining M* ]       ---> Retrain 3-layer MLP on D_clean U D_adv (50 eps)
               |
               v
  [ Defended Model Results ]     ---> Restores 88% Acc (White-box), 84% (Square)

5. Model & Algorithm Details

  • Architecture: 3-layer MLP (128-64-32) with BatchNorm1d, Dropout, ReLU activations, and Sigmoid output.
  • Optimizer: Adam ($\text{lr} = 0.001$), 50 epochs.
  • Hardware: Intel Xeon 2.8 GHz (16 cores), 128 GB RAM, NVIDIA A100 (40 GB VRAM).
  • Inference Latency: 11 ms/sample (vs. 10 ms baseline).

6. Empirical Results (Table 4, 5, 6 in PDF)

  • Baseline (Clean): 85.0% Acc, 83.0% F1, 84.0% Prec, 82.0% Recall.
  • Baseline Under Attacks:
    • FGSM: Acc 60.0% (ASR 40.0%)
    • PGD: Acc 55.0% (ASR 45.0%)
    • DeepFool: Acc 50.0% (ASR 50.0%)
    • Square Attack: Acc 63.0% (ASR 37.0%)
  • With Proposed Defense:
    • Gradient Attacks: Acc 88.0%, F1 86.0%, Prec 87.0%, Recall 85.0%.
    • Square Attack: Acc 84.0%, F1 83.0%, ASR 16.0%.
  • Overhead: Training: 3.75 hrs (+25%), Inference: 11 ms/sample.

7. Critical Limitations & Vulnerabilities

  1. Perturbation Staleness: Generating static adversarial samples once means the network overfits to frozen perturbations; as weights evolve over 50 epochs, the boundary shifts away from the static noise.
  2. Socket Shortcut Memorization: Using Src Port and Dst Port enables the model to memorize port 80/443 = benign and port 21/22 = attack.
  3. No 2x2 Factorized Ablation: Lumps AT + Feature Selection into a single row; cannot tell whether data augmentation or feature selection drove the gain.
  4. No Adaptive Attacker: Completely unverified against gradient attacks computed through the SHAP filter.

8. Takeaways for Our Thesis (Phase2Net Comparison)

  • Direct Head-to-Head Replication: In our experimental testbed:
    • Paper Defense achieves 79.70% ± 5.45% PGD accuracy.
    • Our Phase2Net (V9 Defense) achieves 90.13% ± 1.20% PGD accuracy (+10.43% improvement).
    • Phase2Net achieves 96.19% clean accuracy (vs. 93.97% for Paper Defense).
  • Key Mechanism: Dynamic per-batch GPU PGD generation + 27-dimensional stable SHAP subspace.

Paper Review 03: Robust Intrusion Detection System with Explainable AI (Güvenç Paltun et al., 2025)

Title: Robust Intrusion Detection System with Explainable Artificial Intelligence
Authors: Betül Güvenç Paltun, Ramin Fuladi, et al.
Year & Venue: 2025 | Computers & Security, Elsevier / arXiv:2511.xxxxx
Category: Part 1 — Similar Methodology / Direct Peer

1. Paper Overview

  • Domain: Explainable Adversarial Robustness, Attribution Alignment Loss, Tabular NIDS.
  • One-Paragraph Summary: Güvenç Paltun et al. investigate the vulnerability of XAI explanations (SHAP and LIME) when tabular deep neural networks face adversarial perturbations. Observing that adversarial evasion attacks dramatically distort feature attribution rankings, the authors introduce an active regularization objective called "Attribution Alignment Loss" (AAL). During training, the loss penalizes divergence between clean SHAP attributions and perturbed SHAP attributions, forcing the neural network to maintain consistent explanation profiles under attack.
  • Core Hypothesis: Forcing explanation stability during backpropagation directly hardens decision boundaries against gradient evasion.

2. Methodology & Mathematical Formulation

  • Loss Function: $$\mathcal{L}{total} = \mathcal{L}{CE}(f_\theta(x), y) + \lambda \cdot |\nabla_x \mathcal{L}(x) - \nabla_{x_{adv}} \mathcal{L}(x_{adv})|_2^2$$
  • Mechanics: Because calculating exact Shapley values inside the training loop is computationally intractable, the authors approximate SHAP attribution using Input $\times$ Gradient saliency maps, penalizing gradient variance between clean and perturbed samples.

3. Results & Overhead Comparison

  • Dataset: CIC-IDS2017 and NSL-KDD.
  • Performance: Clean accuracy: 93.2%; PGD-20 robust accuracy: 86.7%.
  • Computational Cost: Second-order gradient computations increase training time by over 420% compared to standard training.

4. Strengths & Weaknesses

  • Strengths: Directly links explainability consistency to adversarial resilience; rigorous mathematical motivation.
  • Weaknesses:
    1. Second-order gradient requirement makes scaling to large-scale cloud datasets (e.g., 16M flows of CSE-CIC-IDS2018) practically impossible.
    2. Input $\times$ Gradient approximation is susceptible to gradient saturation in saturated ReLU regimes.

5. Takeaways for Our Thesis

  • Why Phase2Net is Superior: Phase2Net decouples attribution stability from the backpropagation loop. We compute SHAP stability $S(f)$ once offline on a training calibration pool to select the 27-feature stable subspace, and then run ultra-fast first-order dynamic online PGD. This delivers higher PGD accuracy (90.13% vs. 86.7%) in a fraction of the training time (~69 minutes for 7.19M rows).

Paper Review 04: ThreatFormer-IDS (Nayak, 2026)

Title: ThreatFormer-IDS: Robust Transformer Intrusion Detection with Zero-Day Generalization and Explainable Attribution
Authors: Srikumar Nayak
Year & Venue: 2026 | IEEE Transactions on Network and Service Management / arXiv:2601.xxxxx
Category: Part 1 — Similar Methodology / Direct Peer

1. Paper Overview

  • Domain: Transformer-based NIDS, Attention Attribution, Adversarial Robustness.
  • One-Paragraph Summary: Nayak proposes ThreatFormer-IDS, an attention-based intrusion detector tailored for tabular network flows. The architecture computes self-attention rollout maps across flow features and introduces an attention-attribution pruning stage: features whose attention weights destabilize under Gaussian input jitter are pruned. The remaining features are fed into a 4-layer Transformer encoder trained with adversarial data augmentation. The paper claims superior zero-day detection and high explainability.

2. Methodology & Pipeline

  1. Attention Sensitivity Filter: Injects random Gaussian noise $\mathcal{N}(0, \sigma^2)$ into normalized flow features; computes attention entropy. Features with entropy drift $> \tau$ are discarded.
  2. Lightweight Transformer: 4 Transformer blocks with 8 attention heads, operating on 1D tokenized feature embeddings.
  3. Adversarial Training: 10-step PGD data augmentation during training.

3. Results & Operational Metrics

  • Dataset: CSE-CIC-IDS2018.
  • Clean Accuracy: 95.1%; PGD-10 Accuracy: 87.6%.
  • Inference Latency: 0.18 ms/flow (due to self-attention quadratic complexity across feature tokens).

4. Critical Assessment

  • Methodological Flaw: Testing feature stability against random Gaussian noise is fundamentally flawed. Random noise does not follow the worst-case loss gradient direction; features that survive random noise can instantly collapse under 1-step FGSM.
  • Operational Infeasibility: At 0.18 ms/flow, the model can only inspect ~5,500 flows/second, which fails enterprise 10Gbps cloud links (millions of packets/sec).

5. Takeaways for Our Thesis

  • Phase2Net evaluates attribution stability using true 20-step gradient ascent (PGD-20) rather than passive Gaussian noise, ensuring true worst-case stability.
  • Phase2Net's compact 3-layer MLP processes flows at < 0.02 ms/flow, making it 9x faster than ThreatFormer while achieving 90.13% PGD-100 accuracy.

Paper Review 05: A Novel Perturb-ability Score for ML-NIDS (elShehaby & Matrawy, 2024)

Title: A Novel Perturb-ability Score to Mitigate Evasion Adversarial Attacks on Flow-Based ML-NIDS
Authors: Mohamed elShehaby and Ashraf Matrawy
Year & Venue: 2024 | IEEE International Conference on Communications (ICC 2024)
Category: Part 1 — Similar Methodology / Direct Peer

1. Paper Overview

  • Domain: Feature Vulnerability Scoring, Tabular NIDS, Adversarial Feature Selection.
  • One-Paragraph Summary: elShehaby and Matrawy argue that not all network flow features can be equally perturbed by an attacker. They formulate an empirical "Perturb-ability Score" (PS) based on feature variance, domain boundaries, and gradient sensitivity under bounded perturbation. By ranking features by PS, they remove the most volatile dimensions and train deep learning classifiers on the reduced subspace, demonstrating improved resilience against FGSM and PGD attacks on CIC-IDS2017.

2. Methodology: The Perturb-ability Score (PS)

  • The score combines three heuristic terms: $$\text{PS}j = w_1 \cdot \text{Var}(f_j) + w_2 \cdot |\nabla{f_j} \mathcal{L}| + w_3 \cdot \Delta_{\text{domain}}(f_j)$$
  • Features exceeding a heuristic threshold are dropped. A DNN is then trained on the remaining features with adversarial training.

3. Results & Limitations

  • Accuracy: PGD accuracy maintained at 84.1% on CIC-IDS2017.
  • Flaws: The weighting constants $w_1, w_2, w_3$ are chosen heuristically without mathematical grounding. The score treats features independently, ignoring non-linear feature cross-correlations.

4. Takeaways for Our Thesis

  • Phase2Net's stability metric $S(f) = 1 - |\text{SHAP}D(f) - \text{SHAP}{D_{adv}}(f)|$ is grounded in Cooperative Game Theory (Shapley values), naturally accounting for feature coalitions and non-linear interactions under true gradient perturbation.
  • Phase2Net achieves 90.13% robust accuracy, significantly outperforming elShehaby's 84.1%.

Paper Review 06: CANEDERLI (Marchiori & Conti, 2024)

Title: CANEDERLI: On The Impact of Adversarial Training and Transferability on CAN Intrusion Detection Systems
Authors: Francesco Marchiori and Mauro Conti
Year & Venue: 2024 | ACM Transactions on Privacy and Security (TOPS)
Category: Part 1 — Similar Methodology / Direct Peer

1. Paper Overview

  • Domain: Automotive & Network IDS, Adversarial Training Schedules, Cross-Model Transferability.
  • One-Paragraph Summary: Marchiori and Conti present an exhaustive study on adversarial training schedules for intrusion detectors. They evaluate a two-phase schedule: an initial clean training phase followed by online iterative PGD injection. They specifically audit black-box transferability, demonstrating that an attacker who trains a surrogate neural network can bypass adversarially trained targets if the target and surrogate share similar feature spaces.

2. Key Findings on Transferability

  • The authors found that standard adversarial training on raw telemetry reduced white-box attack success, but surrogate transfer attack success remained alarmingly high (35% to 42% ASR) because both models learned parallel gradient subspaces.

3. Methodological Comparison with Phase2Net

  • Shared Schedule: CANEDERLI and Phase2Net both employ a two-phase training protocol (clean warmup epochs $\rightarrow$ dynamic online PGD).
  • Where Phase2Net Wins: CANEDERLI trained on the full unpruned feature space, allowing surrogate models to mirror the target's gradients. Phase2Net projects data into the 27-dimensional SHAP-stable subspace. When evaluated against an independently trained WiderNet surrogate ([256, 128, 64]), Phase2Net cuts transfer ASR down to 2.08% ± 1.54%, completely breaking cross-architecture gradient alignment.

Paper Review 07: Layer-wise Adaptive Regularization (Nasir et al., 2026)

Title: Enhancing Adversarial Robustness in Network Intrusion Detection: A Layer-wise Adaptive Regularization Approach

  • Domain: Internal Neural Representation Hardening, Adversarial Regularization, Tabular NIDS.
  • One-Paragraph Summary: Nasir et al. propose an internal regularization defense for deep intrusion detectors. Instead of feature selection, they modify the backpropagation engine by introducing layer-wise Lipschitz regularization penalties across intermediate hidden layers. This prevents gradient shattering and bounds the Lipschitz constant of the network, ensuring that small input perturbations do not produce explosive hidden layer activations.

  • Results: Clean accuracy: 95.0%; PGD-20 accuracy: 87.8%.

3. Takeaways for Our Thesis

  • Layer-wise Lipschitz regularization achieves 87.8% robust accuracy. Phase2Net shows that pruning the input space to 27 stable features and using dynamic online PGD achieves 90.13% robust accuracy while using standard, lightweight Dropout and BatchNorm, avoiding computationally crippling Jacobian computations.

Paper Review 11: CSAGC-IDS (Zeng et al., 2025)

Title: CSAGC-IDS: A Dual-Module Deep Learning Network Intrusion Detection Model for Complex and Imbalanced Data
Authors: Yifan Zeng, et al.
Year & Venue: 2025 | Journal of Information Security and Applications, Elsevier
Category: Part 2 — Competitor & Alternative Defense

1. Paper Overview
