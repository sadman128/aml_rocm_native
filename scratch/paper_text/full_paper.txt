ABSTRACT The increasing adoption of cloud-based infrastructures necessitates robust cybersecurity
measures, particularly in Intrusion Detection Systems (IDS). While Machine Learning (ML)-based IDS
solutions improve attack detection, they remain highly vulnerable to adversarial attacks, where subtle
perturbations deceive the model and evade detection. This paper explicitly evaluates the impact of Fast
Gradient Sign Method (FGSM), Projected Gradient Descent (PGD), and DeepFool attacks on ML-based
IDS models trained on the CSE-CIC-IDS2018 dataset. Our findings clearly indicate that these attacks
significantly degrade detection accuracy, with FGSM explicitly reducing accuracy from 85% to 60%, PGD
explicitly to 55%, and DeepFool explicitly to 50%. Additionally, our explicit evaluation using the black-box
Square Attack demonstrates a significant accuracy reduction to 63%, clearly underscoring IDS susceptibility
to practical adversarial threats. To mitigate these vulnerabilities, we explicitly propose a dual-layered defense
strategy: (i) adversarial training, explicitly incorporating adversarial examples into model training to improve
robustness, and (ii) SHAP-based robust feature selection, explicitly enhancing interpretability and resilience
by identifying stable, attack-resistant features. SHAP analysis explicitly reveals that protocol-based and
statistical traffic attributes are particularly susceptible to adversarial manipulations. Explicit computational
overhead analysis demonstrates that our proposed defense approach introduces modest additional training
complexity (approximately 25% overhead) without significantly impacting inference speed, explicitly
confirming practical feasibility for real-time deployment. Our defense mechanisms explicitly restore
detection accuracy to 88% against gradient-based attacks and 84% against black-box attacks, explicitly
reducing adversarial success rates by up to 40%. By explicitly integrating adversarial defenses with SHAP-
based interpretability, this research explicitly strengthens IDS frameworks against diverse evasion attacks
while explicitly enhancing model transparency and practical applicability.

A. ADVERSARIAL THREATS TO CLOUD SECURITY
Recent advancements in adversarial machine learning (AML)
have enabled attackers to craft highly effective evasion
attacks against ML-based IDS. Attackers utilize techniques
such as Fast Gradient Sign Method (FGSM), Projected
Gradient Descent (PGD), and DeepFool to identify decision
boundaries within IDS models and perturb network traffic
in ways that remain undetectable to conventional security
mechanisms [6], [7], [8]. These attacks pose a significant risk
in cloud environments, where IDS models must continuously
adapt to dynamic traffic patterns and evolving attack
methodologies.
The distributed nature of cloud security amplifies the risk
of adversarial threats, as attackers can target centralized IDS
models, federated security frameworks, or even distributed
edge-based intrusion detection systems [5], [11]. Without
effective countermeasures, adversarial vulnerabilities can
lead to data breaches, financial fraud, service disruptions, and
compromised industrial control systems [9]. This necessitates
the development of robust adversarial defense mechanisms
that enhance IDS resilience without significantly increasing
computational overhead [10].
B. CHALLENGES IN ML-BASED CLOUD IDS
Despite their potential, ML-based IDS solutions face several
key challenges in adversarial settings:
• Susceptibility to Adversarial Perturbations: Even
small perturbations in network traffic features can cause
ML models to misclassify benign traffic as malicious or
vice versa, reducing IDS effectiveness [12].
• High False Positive Rates: Anomaly-based IDS
often generate excessive false alarms, leading to alert
fatigue and reduced trust in automated security mech-
anisms [13].
• Computational Overhead: Adversarial training and
other defense techniques impose significant computa-
tional costs, making real-time IDS operation in cloud
environments challenging [14].
• Adaptive Attack Strategies: Adversaries continuously
refine evasion techniques, necessitating dynamic and
adaptive IDS models that can learn from evolving threat
landscapes [15].
• Scalability and Generalization: IDS models trained
on specific datasets may struggle to generalize across
diverse cloud infrastructures and attack scenarios, lim-
iting their real-world effectiveness [16].
Addressing these challenges requires a multi-faceted
approach, combining adversarial training, robust feature
selection, and explainability-driven defense mechanisms.

II. RELATED WORK AND LITERATURE REVIEW
A. EVOLUTION OF INTRUSION DETECTION SYSTEMS
Intrusion Detection Systems (IDS) have undergone signifi-
cant evolution from traditional signature-based methods to
modern AI-driven solutions. Early IDS frameworks relied
on signature-based detection, which involved maintaining a
database of known attack patterns and comparing incoming
traffic against these signatures. While effective against known
threats, these systems struggled to detect novel attacks or
zero-day exploits [19].
Anomaly-based IDS emerged as an alternative, leverag-
ing statistical models and rule-based approaches to detect
deviations from normal behavior. However, these methods
suffered from high false positive rates and the inability to
adapt to evolving cyber threats [20]. With advancements
in machine learning and deep learning, AI-driven IDS
frameworks have gained prominence due to their capability
to learn complex attack patterns and generalize to previously
unseen threats [21].
B. ADVERSARIAL ATTACKS ON ML-BASED IDS
Despite the success of ML-based IDS, adversarial attacks
have been identified as a major vulnerability. Several studies
have demonstrated that attackers can generate adversarial
examples—perturbed versions of legitimate traffic—to fool
IDS models into misclassifying malicious activities as
benign [4].
Goodfellow et al. introduced the Fast Gradient Sign
Method (FGSM), a single-step attack that perturbs input
samples along the gradient direction to deceive ML mod-
els [22]. Madry et al. extended this approach with the
Projected Gradient Descent (PGD) attack, which iteratively
refines adversarial examples for increased effectiveness [23].
Moosavi-Dezfooli et al. proposed DeepFool, an attack that
finds the minimal perturbation required to alter classification.

C. EXISTING DEFENSE MECHANISMS
To counter adversarial attacks, several defense mechanisms
have been explored. Adversarial training, one of the most
effective strategies, involves incorporating adversarial exam-
ples into the training dataset to improve model robustness.
However, adversarial training often comes at the cost of
increased computational complexity and reduced model
generalization [3].
Feature selection has also been studied as a potential
defense against adversarial threats. Researchers have demon-
strated that adversarial attacks exploit certain weak features
within IDS models, making feature selection an essential
step in improving resilience [4]. In addition, ensemble
learning, where multiple models work collectively to enhance
robustness, has been explored as a defense strategy. While
ensemble approaches improve attack resistance, they intro-
duce additional computational overhead and deployment
complexity. We also proposed PRIVFED, a framework
combining Federated Learning with differential privacy
that achieves strong privacy guarantees while maintaining
high model performance. This approach demonstrated that
even with strong privacy protection, the system maintained
88.7% accuracy with only 15.3% computational overhead.
This suggests that privacy-preserving distributed learning
could be effectively applied to IDS systems, particularly in
scenarios where data sensitivity and privacy requirements are
paramount [5].
Recent research has explored hybrid defense approaches
combining adversarial training with anomaly detection
techniques to provide robust protection against known and
unknown threats [25]. Such integrated approaches leverage
adversarial training’s capability to mitigate specific adver-
sarial attacks and anomaly detection’s potential to recognize
deviations from normal patterns, thereby effectively address-
ing zero-day and unknown adversarial threats. However,
explicit evaluations of these hybrid frameworks in practical
cloud-based IDS scenarios remain limited, indicating a
significant research gap.
V arious alternative defense methods have also been
explored in literature, including randomized smoothing,
ensemble defenses, and input transformations. Randomized
smoothing methods introduce noise during inference to
reduce the sensitivity of models to adversarial perturbations;
however, they can degrade detection accuracy and incur sub-
stantial computational costs. Ensemble defenses, combining
multiple classifiers trained independently, typically achieve
better robustness through diversity but significantly increase
computational and operational complexity. Input transforma-
tion techniques (e.g., feature squeezing or denoising) aim to
remove adversarial perturbations before classification but can
introduce unwanted data distortion that negatively impacts
legitimate traffic classification accuracy [26].
In contrast, our selected approach—adversarial train-
ing combined explicitly with SHAP-based robust feature
selection—offers a balanced and practical solution explicitly
addressing adversarial robustness while preserving inter-
pretability, computational efficiency, and maintaining high
accuracy. SHAP integration explicitly ensures interpretability
and robustness at a feature-level, making our method highly
suitable explicitly for deployment in realistic cloud-based
IDS scenarios.
D. GAPS IN EXISTING RESEARCH
Despite extensive research in adversarial attacks and defense
mechanisms, several gaps remain unaddressed:
• Many existing studies focus on static IDS environments,
whereas real-world cloud environments are highly
dynamic and require adaptable defenses.
• Adversarial training methods often degrade model
performance on clean (non-adversarial) samples, neces-
sitating hybrid defense mechanisms.
• There is limited research on the impact of adversarial
attacks in specific industry sectors such as telecommu-
nications, finance, and manufacturing.
• Feature selection techniques need further refinement to
balance interpretability, robustness, and computational
efficiency in IDS models.
This paper aims to bridge these gaps by proposing an
integrated adversarial defense framework that combines
adversarial training with robust feature selection. Our
approach is evaluated on real-world cloud IDS datasets,
demonstrating its effectiveness in mitigating adversarial
threats while preserving model accuracy.

For this study, we utilize the CSE-CIC-IDS2018 dataset,
which is one of the most comprehensive and widely recog-
nized benchmark datasets for evaluating intrusion detection
models. This dataset was chosen due to several key factors
that make it particularly well-suited for adversarial robustness
research:
• Realistic Network Traffic: The dataset is generated
from real-world network traffic, encompassing both
benign and malicious activities that mirror real-world
attack scenarios.
• Wide Range of Attacks: It includes various modern
cyberattacks such as brute force, botnet activity, dis-
tributed denial-of-service (DDoS), SQL injection, and
infiltration attacks, providing a diverse attack landscape
for IDS training and evaluation.
• Feature Richness: The dataset consists of over 80 net-
work flow features, including packet-level statistics,
connection duration, protocol information, and payload
characteristics, allowing for deep analysis of adversarial
vulnerabilities.
• Well-Labeled and Structured: CSE-CIC-IDS2018
provides well-labeled instances of both normal and
attack traffic, facilitating the supervised learning
approach necessary for training ML-based IDS models.
• Cloud Suitability: Given that cloud environments are
highly dynamic and prone to evolving cyber threats, the
dataset captures the complexity of network interactions,
making it a suitable benchmark for cloud IDS evalua-
tion.
By using this dataset, we ensure that our study closely
aligns with real-world IDS deployment scenarios, partic-
ularly in cloud environments where intrusion patterns are
highly dynamic. The dataset’s rich feature set and diverse
attack classes allow us to rigorously test adversarial attack
strategies and validate the effectiveness of our proposed
defense mechanisms.
B. PREPROCESSING AND FEATURE ENGINEERING
Before training our IDS models, we apply extensive data
preprocessing and feature engineering techniques to enhance
learning efficiency and adversarial robustness. The following
steps are undertaken:
• Data Cleaning: Removal of duplicate records, handling
missing values, and normalization of numeric features.
• Feature Selection: Identification and retention of the
most relevant features using mutual information ranking
and variance thresholding.
• Categorical Encoding: One-hot encoding of cate-
gorical variables to ensure compatibility with ML
algorithms.
• Balancing Class Distribution: The dataset is balanced
using synthetic data generation techniques such as
SMOTE (Synthetic Minority Over-sampling Technique)
to prevent bias toward majority classes.
These preprocessing steps ensure that our models receive
high-quality input, reducing the likelihood of bias and
improving the resilience of IDS frameworks against adver-
sarial attacks.
C. ADVERSARIAL ATTACK IMPLEMENTATION
To evaluate the vulnerability of ML-based IDS, we imple-
ment three widely used adversarial attack techniques:
• FGSM (Fast Gradient Sign Method): Generates
adversarial perturbations by computing the gradient of
the loss function and applying a single-step modification
to the input data. This is a fast and computationally
efficient attack that exploits linearity in ML models.
• PGD (Projected Gradient Descent): An iterative
attack that refines adversarial examples by applying
multiple gradient updates, making it more effective in
generating adversarial samples that deceive the IDS.
• DeepFool: A minimal perturbation attack that iteratively
shifts input features until they cross the decision
boundary of the IDS, ensuring high attack success rates
with subtle modifications to the input data.
These attacks are applied to the IDS model to evaluate its
robustness and inform the development of stronger defensive
mechanisms.
D. DEFENSE STRATEGIES
To counteract the impact of adversarial attacks, we incorpo-
rate two primary defense mechanisms:
• Adversarial Training: By integrating adversarially
generated samples into the training process, we enhance
the model’s ability to recognize and withstand adversar-
ial inputs, improving its overall robustness.
• Robust Feature Selection: Certain features in network
traffic data are more susceptible to adversarial manip-
ulations. By selecting and prioritizing features that are
stable and less prone to perturbations, we improve the
resilience of the IDS model.

While our evaluation explicitly focuses on adversarial

V. FEATURE IMPORTANCE ANALYSIS USING SHAP
One of the critical aspects of improving adversarial robust-
ness in ML-based IDS is understanding the role of individual
features in classification decisions. Feature importance anal-
ysis helps identify which network traffic attributes contribute
most to the IDS model’s predictions and how adversarial
attacks manipulate these features to evade detection. In this
study, we employ SHAP (SHapley Additive exPlanations)
to quantify feature importance and analyze how adversarial
perturbations affect IDS decision boundaries.
A. METHODOLOGY
SHAP provides an interpretable way to assess the influence of
each feature on model predictions by computing the average
marginal contribution of a feature across different subsets of
input data. The key steps in our feature importance analysis
are:
• Train an ML-based IDS model on the CSE-CIC-
IDS2018 dataset.
• Generate adversarial samples using FGSM, PGD, and
DeepFool.
• Compute SHAP values for both benign and adversarial
samples.
• Compare SHAP values before and after attack perturba-
tions to determine which features are most affected.
B. FEATURE DESCRIPTIONS AND IMPORTANCE
The SHAP analysis focuses on key network traffic
attributes, evaluating how adversarial perturbations impact
their relative importance in IDS decision-making. Below is a
description of the most influential features:
• Protocol - Indicates the network protocol (e.g., TCP ,
UDP , ICMP). Different protocols exhibit distinct traffic
behaviors, making this a crucial feature for attack
detection.
• Src Port (Source Port)- Represents the originating port
number in network communication, often manipulated
in port-scanning and flooding attacks.
• Dst Port (Destination Port) - The target port for
network traffic, where attacks frequently target known
service vulnerabilities.
• Flow Duration - Measures the duration of a connection.
Adversaries often manipulate connection timing to
evade detection.
• Packet Length - Represents the size of network packets.
V ariations in packet length distribution can indicate
malicious activity.
• Flow Bytes/s - The rate of data transmission within a
connection. Attackers may throttle or amplify this metric
to evade detection.
• Flow Packets/s - The number of packets transmit-
ted per second. High-frequency, short-duration traf-
fic patterns are common in DDoS and brute-force
attacks.
• Fwd Packet Length - The length of packets sent in the
forward direction. Attackers may use inconsistencies in
packet length to disguise malicious activity.
These features play a critical role in IDS classification,
and adversarial attacks attempt to exploit their vulnerabilities
by either perturbing high-contribution features or amplifying
irrelevant attributes.

Our feature importance analysis highlights the need for

training, future models can:
• Identify features that remain consistent under attack
conditions, reducing adversarial susceptibility.
• Improve model interpretability by providing insights
into feature stability.
• Enhance generalization by selecting features that retain
predictive power across different attack scenarios.
By leveraging SHAP-based feature selection alongside
adversarial training, our proposed IDS framework signifi-
cantly enhances security, interpretability, and robustness,
ensuring improved resilience in detecting and mitigating
adversarial cyber threats in cloud environments.
VI. DISCUSSION AND ANALYSIS
Our experimental findings demonstrate that adversarial
attacks significantly degrade the performance of ML-based
IDS models. FGSM, PGD, and DeepFool attacks reduced
classification accuracy by up to 40%, underscoring the
vulnerability of IDS frameworks deployed in cloud environ-
ments. However, our proposed adversarial training and robust
feature selection strategies effectively mitigated these threats,
restoring model accuracy and reducing attack success rates.
The evaluation against the Square Attack further con-
firms our framework’s generalization capability, highlighting
that the integrated defense strategy not only mitigates
gradient-based adversarial threats but also provides meaning-
ful robustness against black-box adversarial scenarios. This
broader robustness is crucial for real-world IDS deployments,
where attackers typically have limited knowledge of IDS
internal workings.
Our computational overhead analysis explicitly demon-
strates that while the dual-layered defense strategy intro-
duces additional training complexity (approximately 25%),
inference speed remains suitable for real-time operations
(11 ms/sample). This modest overhead, comparable to
existing methods like PRIVFED [5], confirms the practical
viability of our framework. Furthermore, potential adoption
of federated learning architectures could distribute computa-
tional overhead across edge devices, significantly enhancing
scalability and computational efficiency for large-scale IDS
deployments.
A. EFFECTIVENESS OF DEFENSE MECHANISMS
Adversarial training proved to be a highly effective counter-
measure, as it exposed the model to adversarial examples
during training, enhancing its resilience against attacks.
Nonetheless, adversarial training increased computational
overhead, highlighting the trade-off between security and
efficiency. Robust feature selection further bolstered model
security by eliminating redundant or easily exploitable
features, thereby improving robustness without sacrificing
computational efficiency.
B. FEATURE IMPORTANCE INSIGHTS FROM SHAP
To gain deeper insights into how adversarial perturba-
tions affect IDS decision-making, we conducted a feature
importance analysis using SHAP . This analysis provided
valuable information regarding the features most impacted
by adversarial attacks and the effectiveness of defense
mechanisms.
• SHAP analysis revealed that protocol-based features and
statistical attributes (e.g., packet rate, duration, and flow
bytes) had the highest SHAP values, indicating their
significance in classification decisions.
• Adversarial attacks primarily targeted high-importance
features such as source/destination IP entropy and con-
nection duration, which are critical for distinguishing
normal and malicious traffic.
• After applying robust feature selection, SHAP values
indicated that selected features exhibited greater sta-
bility under adversarial conditions, improving model
robustness.

C. LIMITATIONS AND CHALLENGES
While our proposed defense mechanisms enhance adversarial
robustness, certain challenges remain:
• Computational Overhead: The inclusion of adversar-
ial training increases training time and computational
cost, which may not be feasible for resource-constrained
cloud environments.
• Adaptive Adversarial Strategies: Attackers can
develop adaptive techniques that bypass adversarially
trained models, necessitating continuous updates and
retraining.
• Feature Importance Variability: SHAP analysis
revealed that some features have high variance in
importance scores across different attacks, making them
potential targets for adversarial exploitation.
• Generalization Across Cloud Platforms: Our frame-
work was evaluated on CSE-CIC-IDS2018; however,
further validation on diverse cloud infrastructures is
required.
Our adversarial evaluation assumes functional integrity
of the adversarially perturbed traffic; however, explicit
validation of this functionality was beyond the scope of this
research. It remains important to validate in practice that
adversarial perturbations do not unintentionally compromise
the execution of malicious activities, as this may otherwise
lead to an overestimation of threat impact. Future research
should explicitly evaluate and validate the functional integrity
of adversarial examples, further enhancing the practical
relevance and threat accuracy assessment of adversarial
robustness studies. Our explicit use of binary classification
provides valuable foundational insights into IDS robustness
but lacks the granularity of multi-class detection necessary
for distinguishing between specific attack types. Future work
should explicitly evaluate the proposed defense methods
using multi-class classification scenarios, providing deeper
insights into the effectiveness of adversarial defenses across
varied attack classes, thereby significantly enhancing their
practical relevance and deployment value.
Detection-based defenses, such as MagNet [27], explicitly
complement our adversarial training and robust feature selec-
tion approaches by providing explicit detection capabilities
at inference time. While our current approach emphasizes
robustness through model training and interpretability-driven
feature selection, explicitly integrating detection-based meth-
ods represents an important and complementary direction for
future research. Our evaluations were explicitly performed
using the CSE-CIC-IDS2018 dataset. While this dataset is
comprehensive, explicitly validating our defense framework
on additional datasets such as UNSW-NB15 or NSL-KDD
would provide stronger evidence of generalizability. Explic-
itly extending evaluations to additional benchmark datasets
remains an essential and explicit future research direction.
D. FEDERATED LEARNING FOR FUTURE RESEARCH
Federated Learning (FL) represents a compelling paradigm
to further enhance adversarial robustness and adaptability in
IDS frameworks. By leveraging decentralized training across
multiple edge nodes, FL can aggregate diverse adversarial
examples collected from various network environments,
providing richer and more representative datasets for training
adaptive defenses. This decentralized approach can dynam-
ically adapt IDS models to emerging threats in real-time,
effectively addressing adaptive adversarial strategies by con-
tinuously incorporating novel attack patterns encountered in
different cloud infrastructures or edge devices. Furthermore,
FL’s distributed computation paradigm inherently reduces
centralized computational overhead, improving scalability
and practical feasibility for large-scale deployments.
By leveraging decentralized training, FL can also
explicitly:
• Reduce data exposure, mitigating privacy concerns
associated with cloud-based IDS.
• Improve model adaptability across heterogeneous cloud
infrastructures.
• Enhance IDS robustness by training on diverse attack
patterns from distributed sources.
• Utilize SHAP-driven feature selection to dynamically
adjust IDS models based on shifting adversarial strate-
gies.
Integrating FL with adversarial defense mechanisms can
further fortify cloud IDS solutions against evolving cyber
threats, making it a compelling avenue for future research.
VII. CONCLUSION
In this study, we examined the adversarial robustness of
ML-based IDS in cloud environments and proposed an inte-
grated defense strategy combining adversarial training and
robust feature selection. Our experimental results explicitly
demonstrated that adversarial attacks significantly compro-
mise IDS accuracy; however, our proposed dual-layered
defense techniques significantly mitigate these threats, restor-
ing and even enhancing model accuracy.
Our findings have critical implications for industries reliant
on cloud security, including telecommunications, finance,
healthcare, and critical infrastructure. By enhancing IDS
resilience, organizations can better safeguard cloud-based
systems against evolving adversarial threats. Moreover, our
SHAP-based feature importance analysis explicitly provided
deeper insights into how adversarial perturbations manipulate
