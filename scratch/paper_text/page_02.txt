A critical consideration regarding adversarial attacks on
network traffic is practical feasibility. Adversarial perturba-
tions in network-based IDS must explicitly adhere to realistic
constraints—such as preserving packet structures, protocol
compliance, timing consistency, and avoiding detectable
anomalies—to remain undetected and functional in real-
world scenarios. Thus, attackers must carefully balance per-
turbation magnitude to evade IDS detection while ensuring
that manipulated traffic maintains its malicious functionality
and network-level plausibility.
A critical aspect of evaluating adversarial threats to IDS
models is understanding whether adversarially perturbed
network traffic retains its functional integrity—specifically,
whether it remains capable of executing the originally
intended malicious activities. In our evaluation, adversarial
attacks (FGSM, PGD, and DeepFool) introduce subtle
feature perturbations that do not alter fundamental traffic
functionality. These perturbations specifically target features
used by IDS classifiers without disrupting packet structure
or network-level semantics, thereby preserving the feasi-
bility of executing functional attacks. Explicit validation
of functional integrity is assumed in our adversarial threat
modeling to reflect realistic adversarial scenarios where
attackers aim to evade detection without sacrificing malicious
effectiveness.
While previous studies have explored adversarial train-
ing [3] and robust feature selection techniques indepen-
dently [4], the integration of these methods into a unified
defense framework has received limited attention. Specifi-
cally, our approach leverages SHAP-based feature analysis
not merely for interpretability but as a critical tool to
systematically identify robust features that remain stable
under adversarial perturbations. Unlike PRIVFED [5], which
primarily focuses on federated learning and privacy enhance-
ments, our framework directly enhances adversarial robust-
ness by combining adversarial training with SHAP-driven
robust feature selection. This dual-layered defense strat-
egy thus provides a unique contribution by simultane-
ously addressing adversarial vulnerabilities and maintain-
ing interpretability—attributes essential for real-world IDS
deployments in cloud environments.
A. ADVERSARIAL THREATS TO CLOUD SECURITY
Recent advancements in adversarial machine learning (AML)
have enabled attackers to craft highly effective evasion
attacks against ML-based IDS. Attackers utilize techniques
such as Fast Gradient Sign Method (FGSM), Projected
Gradient Descent (PGD), and DeepFool to identify decision
boundaries within IDS models and perturb network traffic
in ways that remain undetectable to conventional security
mechanisms [6], [7], [8]. These attacks pose a significant risk
in cloud environments, where IDS models must continuously
adapt to dynamic traffic patterns and evolving attack
methodologies.
The distributed nature of cloud security amplifies the risk
of adversarial threats, as attackers can target centralized IDS
models, federated security frameworks, or even distributed
edge-based intrusion detection systems [5], [11]. Without
effective countermeasures, adversarial vulnerabilities can
lead to data breaches, financial fraud, service disruptions, and
compromised industrial control systems [9]. This necessitates
the development of robust adversarial defense mechanisms
that enhance IDS resilience without significantly increasing
computational overhead [10].
B. CHALLENGES IN ML-BASED CLOUD IDS
Despite their potential, ML-based IDS solutions face several
key challenges in adversarial settings:
• Susceptibility to Adversarial Perturbations: Even
small perturbations in network traffic features can cause
ML models to misclassify benign traffic as malicious or
vice versa, reducing IDS effectiveness [12].
• High False Positive Rates: Anomaly-based IDS
often generate excessive false alarms, leading to alert
fatigue and reduced trust in automated security mech-
anisms [13].
• Computational Overhead: Adversarial training and
other defense techniques impose significant computa-
tional costs, making real-time IDS operation in cloud
environments challenging [14].
• Adaptive Attack Strategies: Adversaries continuously
refine evasion techniques, necessitating dynamic and
adaptive IDS models that can learn from evolving threat
landscapes [15].
• Scalability and Generalization: IDS models trained
on specific datasets may struggle to generalize across
diverse cloud infrastructures and attack scenarios, lim-
iting their real-world effectiveness [16].
Addressing these challenges requires a multi-faceted
approach, combining adversarial training, robust feature
selection, and explainability-driven defense mechanisms.
